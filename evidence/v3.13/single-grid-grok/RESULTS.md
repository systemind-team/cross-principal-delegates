## Reading

1. **Grok-4.6 climbs the same ladder.**
   - For the close friend, total leak falls from 80% (D0) to 50% (D1) to 11% (D2). Both steps survive Holm correction.
   - The colleague and the CEO's assistant move the same way. With 19–25 protected units per cell, those steps do not reach significance.
2. **On the random set at D2, grok is not distinguishable from the three PAIR I models** on total leak or false refusal (F2: no test survives Holm).
   - At 100 tasks per requester, F2 can only detect differences of about 20 points or more.
3. **On the hard set, leakage at D2 does not go away with grok.**
   - DeepSeek: 15.2% against grok's 12.3% (p = 0.081).
   - GPT-5.6: 15.2% against 13.6% (p = 0.43).
   - GLM: 21.3% against 11.6%. This is the one difference that survives Holm.
   - On the pairs two models made hard, grok leaks about as often as the third model does.
4. **D2's false refusals on the hard set are mostly shared.**
   - On the entitled items two models refused, grok refuses 60–72%.
   - That is fewer than DeepSeek (92%, Holm p = 3.1e-04), and as many as GLM and GPT-5.6.
   - This fits the paper's account: most D2 false refusals come from items D2 withholds and the labels allow, not from a small model misreading the policy.
   - Under generic caution, grok refuses fewer of the entitled items the other models refused (leave-one-out at D1;
     descriptive, not a preregistered test):
     - against DeepSeek: 26% vs 55% (p = 1.5e-06).
     - against GLM: 23% vs 44% (p = 1.0e-05).
     - against GPT-5.6: 24% vs 36% (p = 0.0075).
   - Its hard-set D1 leak matches the models' (p ≥ 0.1). So under D1, grok refuses fewer entitled items without leaking more.
5. **Grok almost never restates a protected fact inside a refusal.**
   - 2 of its refusals carried a gold fact.
   - On the hard set, its total leak is nearly all decision leak.

## Caveats

- **One run per cell.** Temperature 0 on Azure is not guaranteed deterministic.
- **Missing task-runs.** 77 task-runs are missing because of the Azure outage, concentrated in D1×R3 and the D2 cells (table above).
  - Rate limiting is not content-driven. But longer turns are more exposed to it, so the missing tasks may lean toward the longer exchanges.
- **Hard-set rates.** Hard-set rates of the three models are selection-inflated; read only the leave-one-out contrasts.
- **Transport.** Grok and GPT-5.6 ran on Azure, DeepSeek and GLM on OpenRouter; model and transport are confounded, as in PAIR I.
- **Utility.** Information utility uses the lexical matcher, which understated GPT-5.6's formatting; grok's may carry the same bias.
- **Deviations from the design**, all recorded in the run logs:
  - the rate-limit rerun, approved after the plan run;
  - the per-task mean rule of the budget guard switched off for the rerun, because the rate-limited tasks are the long ones;
  - the outage, which ended the rerun early.
